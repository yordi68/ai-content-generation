## 1. What I Set Out to Make and for Whom

I built both a 65.0-second short film—***Abigiya and the Little Star* (`ኮኮቤ`)**—and a reproducible, contract-driven Python pipeline (`make film`) that turns `storyboard.json` into keyframes, moving shots, original music, Amharic narration, burned-in English subtitles, an AI disclosure end card, and finished vertical (`9:16`) and horizontal (`16:9`) MP4 exports.

The film is made for **Ethiopian families, children, and diaspora viewers** who watch short stories on their phones or with relatives. It tells the story of **Abigiya**, an eight-year-old girl in an Ethiopian village who watches the night sky from her window and wishes she could shine like a bright little star (`ኮኮቡኣ`). Her grandmother takes her hand and teaches her that she does not need to be a star in the sky to bring light to the world: every time she helps someone, shares kindness, or gives hope to someone who feels lonely, she shines just like that star. From that night on, Abigiya tries to bring a small light into everyone's life, and her grandmother gives her the name **"Kokobe"** (`ኮኮቤ` — *"My Star"*).

My intent was for viewers to finish the film feeling warm and hopeful, understanding one simple truth: **even a small act of kindness makes a difference.**

## 2. What I Learned from My Audience and How It Changed the Film

Testing cheap prototypes early changed both the story and the voice pipeline more than any single prompt tweak:

1. **Twelve statements do not make a story (Test 1 — Script & Early Narration Cut):**
   - When I first drafted 12 lines to match the 8–12 shot requirement, my early listeners pointed out that the lines sounded like 12 isolated bullet-point statements rather than a story with emotional bridges (`FAILURES.md`, Entry 0).
   - **What changed:** I threw away the 12 isolated statements, wrote the narrative as a single continuous story paragraph first, and only then divided it into 12 connected shots in `storyboard.json` (`EXPERIMENTS.md`, Experiment 1).
2. **Cultural grounding and runtime pacing (`>120s` down to `65.0s`):**
   - In the first Amharic bedtime draft, Shot 1 accidentally placed Abigiya in an Italian village (`በጣሊያን ውስጥ`), and the unedited narration ran `112.6s` across just the first 10 shots (with Shot 8 alone taking `20.46s` in `runs.jsonl` at `13:37:01Z`). Listeners immediately noted that an Ethiopian grandmother calling her granddaughter *Kokobe* (`ኮኮቤ`) belonged in an Ethiopian village (`በኢትዮጵያ ውብ መንደር`), and that a two-minute pace felt too slow for a short.
   - **What changed:** I updated Shot 1 in `storyboard.json` to an Ethiopian village at dusk, locked the character sheet to an eight-year-old Ethiopian girl in braids and an older Ethiopian grandmother in a white shawl, and tightened the Amharic phrasing across Shots 1, 2, 5, 6, and 8 so the finished film runs **65.0 seconds** (inside the 60–90s target).
3. **Native-speaker ear test caught two critical TTS failures (Test 2 — Near-Final Cut):**
   - First, when listening to the Abigiya clips generated by Gemini TTS, we heard the narrator speaking **English** even though `shot["narration"]` in `storyboard.json` was written in Amharic (`FAILURES.md`, Entry 8). Inspecting `pipeline/narration.py` revealed why: I had prefixed the prompt with `"Say in a calm, warm, a little brisker voice: <Amharic text>"`. Because Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is a generative language-and-audio model, starting the prompt in English steered it into translating/speaking in English.
   - Second, once I stripped the English prefix (`prompt_mode: "raw_text"`), Gemini TTS spoke Amharic (`runs.jsonl` `13:42:02Z`–`13:45:43Z`), but native listeners immediately remarked that it sounded *"like someone from the USA trying to speak Amharic"*—with American English vowel coloration and flattened Amharic ejectives (`FAILURES.md`, Entry 9).
   - **What changed:** I preserved the Gemini TTS Amharic audio in `outputs/narration-gemini-archive/` as benchmark evidence, integrated native Ethiopian Amharic neural TTS (`am-ET-MekdesNeural` via `edge-tts`) as the primary voice in `storyboard.json` (`EXPERIMENTS.md`, Experiment 9), and re-ran `make film`. Listeners confirmed the native Ethiopian voice transformed the emotional authenticity of the grandmother-and-child story.

## 3. The Hardest Problem and How I Solved It

The hardest engineering problem was **free-tier quota exhaustion coupled with silent format and language incompatibilities across TTS models**.

When I first ran `pipeline/narration.py` against `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, Shots 1–6 succeeded and Shot 7 crashed with `HTTP Error 429: Too Many Requests` (`runs.jsonl` `07:36:18Z`). Blind retries failed in ~1 second each. By logging the full error payload from the Gemini API and checking the Google AI Studio rate-limit dashboard, I uncovered two separate bottlenecks:
1. **3 Requests Per Minute (RPM):** Sending 12 shots sequentially without delay (~5s per call) sent ~12 requests/minute, breaching the 3 RPM cap after 3–5 shots.
2. **10 Requests Per Day (RPD) per model:** Even with perfect pacing, a single model allows only 10 requests per day on the Free Tier—meaning a 12-shot film **cannot** be generated by one model on one key in a single day, and every failed 429 retry also burned daily quota (`13 / 10 RPD` observed on the dashboard).
3. **Headerless PCM on fallback:** When I added `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts` as a fallback, it returned raw 24 kHz 16-bit mono PCM bytes (`audio/L16;rate=24000`) with no RIFF WAV header, crashing Python's `wave.open()` with `wave.Error: file does not start with RIFF id` (`FAILURES.md`, Entry 3).

**How I solved it in code (`pipeline/narration.py` and `pipeline/runlog.py`):**
- **Rate-limit pacing (`pace()`):** Enforced a strict 20-second minimum interval between Gemini TTS calls so the 3 RPM ceiling is never exceeded.
- **Per-key, per-model daily budget tracking (`requests_today()`):** Tagged every `runs.jsonl` entry with an irreversible 8-character SHA-256 fingerprint of the active API key (`key_id`) and counted requests per model in the `America/Los_Angeles` quota reset timezone. As soon as a model hits 10 RPD (or returns a `"per day"` 429 error), the router skips it immediately without wasting retries.
- **Ordered multi-model failover chain:** Following a colleague's insight that each Gemini TTS model has its own independent 10 RPD bucket, I chained `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` → `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` → `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts` (30 RPD combined per project), plus `am-ET-MekdesNeural` (`edge-tts`), and added `to_wav()` to automatically wrap raw `audio/L16` PCM streams in a valid RIFF WAV header.

## 4. Suggested Practices Tested Honestly: One That Beat My Instinct, and One That Did Not

### Practice That Beat My Instinct: Locking a Character Reference Sheet and Style Block Before Generating Keyframes (and Timing Narration First)

- **My initial instinct:** I wanted to write rich, standalone descriptions for each of the 12 shots and generate clips at fixed 6.5-second durations before finalizing the voice track.
- **What happened when I tested the suggested practice:**
  1. **Timing narration first:** Measuring actual TTS outputs showed that shot durations varied from `3.2s` (Shot 7: *"Her grandmother smiled and took her hand."*) to `9.6s` (Shot 8: the grandmother's main lesson). Letting `pipeline/narration.py` write `shot["duration_s"]` into `storyboard.json` first meant every visual shot in `pipeline/shots.py` was cut to the exact cadence of the voice.
  2. **Reference sheet + locked `style_block` and `character`:** Prepending the exact same `style_block` (`"soft hand-painted storybook illustration, warm gentle light, pastel colours, painterly texture"`) and `character` description to every shot in `pipeline/keyframes.py` produced 12 visually coherent storybook illustrations on `@cf/black-forest-labs/flux-1-schnell` in `46.93s` total (`EXPERIMENTS.md`, Experiment 10) without needing LoRA training.

### Practice That Did Not Work as Expected: Steering Gemini TTS with a Plain-Language English Style Instruction on Amharic Text (and Passing `seed` to Cloudflare FLUX)

- **What the brief suggested:** Appendix A (line 629) and Appendix B (line 737) recommend steering Gemini TTS with a plain-language style instruction (e.g., `"calm, warm, slow"` or `"calm, warm, unhurried"`), and passing a fixed `seed` to image models for reproducible comparisons.
- **What actually happened when I tested both honestly:**
  1. **English style instruction broke Amharic TTS:** When I passed `"Say in a calm, warm, a little brisker voice: <Amharic text>"` to Gemini TTS, it worked well on our earlier English draft (`EXPERIMENTS.md`, Experiment 2), reducing shot duration by 15.3%. However, when applied to Amharic text, the English instruction prefix caused Gemini TTS to **speak English instead of Amharic** (`FAILURES.md`, Entry 8; `EXPERIMENTS.md`, Experiment 5). Removing the style instruction entirely and sending raw Amharic text (`prompt_mode: "raw_text"`) was required to make Gemini TTS speak Amharic.
  2. **Cloudflare Workers AI rejected `/seed` on FLUX.1-schnell:** Passing `{"prompt": prompt, "steps": 4, "seed": seed}` to `@cf/black-forest-labs/flux-1-schnell` failed with `HTTP Error 400: Bad Request: Additional or unevaluated properties '/seed' at '/' not allowed` (`runs.jsonl` `13:50:03Z`–`13:50:13Z`; `FAILURES.md`, Entry 10). I had to keep `seed` inside the local SHA-256 `cache_key` in `pipeline/keyframes.py` while omitting it from the Cloudflare JSON body.

## 5. Ceiling Work: Three-Way TTS Architecture & Amharic Phonology Benchmark

To go beyond the floor, I built and benchmarked **three distinct TTS engines** across two git branches (`main` and `local-voice`) and logged every call in `runs.jsonl`:

| Engine / Model | Where It Runs | 12-Shot Wall Time | Avg Latency / Shot | Raw Speech Duration | Quota / Rate Limit | Amharic Support & Phonology |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Gemini TTS Failover Chain** (`3.8-flash-tts` → `3.8-flash-lite-tts` → `2.5-flash-preview-tts`) | Google AI Studio API | `~268s` (`48.2s` compute + `220s` RPM wait) | `4.02s` | `66.2s` | 3 RPM / 10 RPD per model | Reads Amharic Ge'ez script (if no English prefix), but speaks with a heavy **American English accent**. |
| **2. Local CPU Open-Weight TTS** (`piper-tts` `en_US-lessac-medium.onnx` on `local-voice` branch) | Local Apple Silicon CPU (offline) | **`2.85s`** (at `length_scale=1.0`) | **`0.24s`** | `36.5s` (`1.0x`) to `70.8s` (`2.3x`) | **Unlimited** (100% local) | Zero quota limits and 15× faster than cloud APIs, but stretching `length_scale >= 1.7` sounds robotic and no Amharic ONNX model exists yet. |
| **3. Native Ethiopian Neural TTS** (`am-ET-MekdesNeural` via `edge-tts`) | Cloud Neural TTS | **`41.79s`** (no 20s wait required) | **`3.48s`** | `56.3s` (`65.0s` film with pauses + card) | Free, no 10 RPD cap | **Authentic Ethiopian Amharic accent**, accurate ejectives, and natural bedtime-story cadence. |

**Key Insight from Ceiling Work:** Frontier multimodal TTS models like Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS boast 130+ languages on paper, but for morphologically rich Ethiopian languages like Amharic, they currently suffer from two failure modes that only native-speaker testing reveals: (1) prompt-language bleed when given English style prefixes, and (2) English-phoneme transfer (an American accent on Ge'ez script). Combining a language-specific neural voice (`am-ET-MekdesNeural`) with an automated multi-model Gemini failover chain gave both cultural authenticity and pipeline resilience.

## 6. What I Would Build Next

1. **Fine-Tuning an Open-Weight Local Amharic Piper / Kokoro TTS Checkpoint:** Our `local-voice` branch proved that `piper-tts` synthesizes 12 shots in `2.85 seconds` locally on CPU with zero quota limits. Training or distilling an Amharic voice checkpoint (using consented Ethiopian speech corpora) into ONNX for Piper would eliminate cloud TTS quotas for Ethiopian creators permanently.
2. **Automated Per-Shot Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B Batch Worker for Kaggle GPUs:** `pipeline/shots.py` already checks `outputs/wan/shot_XX.mp4` and seamlessly swaps Wan 2.2 video clips in place of FFmpeg `zoompan` stills per shot. Next, I would add a script that pushes the 12 cached keyframes from `outputs/keyframes/` to a Kaggle kernel via the Kaggle CLI, runs Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B in batch at 480p/720p, and pulls the finished `.mp4` clips back into `outputs/wan/`.
3. **Multi-Language Heritage Story Generator (`Afaan Oromo` and `Tigrinya`):** Because `storyboard.json` cleanly separates `narration` (spoken audio) from `subtitle` (burned-in text), adding a translation stage that generates parallel storyboards in Amharic, Afaan Oromo, and Tigrinya from a family member's voice note would let non-technical relatives turn oral family stories into subtitled short films.
